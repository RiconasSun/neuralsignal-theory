# Observation, timing and artifact contract

This M0 accepts **conventional curated-release observations**. It does not certify absolute lossless hardware acquisition and does not require that certificate. It also does not infer artifact-free recordings from missing annotations.

For the two development pilots, recorded AP sample counts follow `fileSizeBytes/(2*nSavedChans)`, agree with `fileTimeSecs * imSampRate`, and are mapped using the original probe-to-session sync table. Both sync tables are finite and strictly increasing and extend beyond the recorded duration; no support endpoint extrapolation is needed. The resulting session-clock bounds are approximately [-0.000065, 5822.012906] and [-0.000039, 5155.718721] seconds. These bounds derive from acquisition metadata and provider synchronization, **not first/last detected spikes**. All candidate native stimulus [0,0.5) windows fit in the respective recorded bounds.

The uploaded `spikes.times` are provider-synchronized session seconds under ALF convention. Actual finite/order/extent checks passed for 34,166,347 and 25,836,824 spikes. Probe-local cluster IDs, UUIDs, metrics and channel-linked atlas maps are internally consistent. This establishes usable conventional event joins, not an independent remeasurement of raw sync residuals or histological truth.

Known AP saturation sample intervals were loaded (716 / 48 intervals), divided by the header's 30 kHz sample rate and mapped through the provider sync. Conservative trial exclusion removes any [stim,stim+0.5) overlap. This excludes 163 / 23 structurally valid windows. The surviving native windows are 940 / 761, with chronological half counts 492/448 and 383/378. Total stimulus-window exposure is 470 / 380.5 seconds. The selected 10-trial counting examples are entirely outside known saturation intervals. These intervals describe known detector outputs, not all possible motion artifacts. Other unannotated artifact burden remains unknown. M1 should freeze a justified saturation-neighborhood sensitivity and report coverage before model outcomes; M0 does not invent a post-outcome exclusion radius.

Valid observations here mean finite, ordered, identity-mapped curated counts inside these supported windows after known-invalid masking. Their physiological quality remains the provider label sampling frame, with further training-only admission deferred to M1. Known-invalid observations are explicitly excluded/masked and never encoded as zero spikes. Essential timing/source contradictions would stop M0. None was found in these two pilots; unknown packet-loss burden is a limitation, not an invented contradiction.

Task go cue, stimulus, feedback, response, contrast/choice and native first movement are present. Wheel timestamps are finite, strictly increasing and cover all usable windows. A strict-past lookup can join wheel observations at bin start; future filtered/interpolated wheel values may not enter a causal predictor. Native first movement is source-derived using the full behavioral processing pipeline: retrospectively selecting movement-aligned windows defines offline event-conditioned prediction, not online movement forecasting.

Old aggregate bwm/saturation flags were used **only for prospective metadata capacity**. They were not copied into current native pilot masks. Actual pilot masks use native task structural integrity and mapped native known saturation. Different mask definitions explain why native usable counts (940/761) exceed old aggregate candidate counts (910/594); this is not a relaxed old gate or retrospective recovery. Future M1 must choose/freeze one native observation/QC policy for every animal before fitting.

The 20 candidates are not 20 admitted animals. Only two development sessions were checked at neural-array level. Provider good labels use whole-session spike sorting metrics; they are source sampling criteria, not newly claimed calibration-only QC. Remaining development/final native annotations, behavior coverage and training/calibration-only physiological QC can reduce coverage. The 8-mouse reservation is not yet a powered confirmatory study. Mouse-level independence does not remove shared-lab/provider effects, sorting errors, anatomy boundary uncertainty or unrecorded movement confounds.
