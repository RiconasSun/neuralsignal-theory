## M4 — LOCAL DYNAMICAL REPRESENTATIONS

Compare two tractable primary model families:

D — Discrete state:
Small-state Poisson HMM using exact log-space forward prediction, not future-state smoothing.

C — Continuous state:
Low-dimensional, fixed-timescale leaky-history state driven only by past observed spikes, with a Poisson predictive readout.

Both must score current counts before assimilating them and account for the registered task/time nuisance structure.

Prioritize analytically tractable likelihoods and bounded parameter optimization.

Use:
- Independent small-scale numerical oracles
- Causal future-perturbation tests
- Exact score-denominator verification
- Fixed seed/configuration rules
- Model-complexity and parameter-budget reporting
- Training-only numerical diagnosis

Pre-register how numerically invalid configurations are handled.

Never remove failed fits simply to favor a model family.

A valid baseline-only result cannot be described as a completed discrete-versus-continuous comparison.

Do not make high-dimensional recurrent networks or difficult latent continuous integration mandatory.

## M5 — SCIENTIFIC COMPARISON

Primary measurement:

Held-out incremental predictive log likelihood relative to the strongest registered task/history baseline.

Units:
nats per actual scored nominal unit-bin.

Evaluate:
- Discrete versus strong baseline
- Continuous versus strong baseline
- Discrete versus continuous, where both are valid

Freeze model selection using development data only.

Use equal-mouse aggregation, paired subject-level inference, confidence intervals and preregistered multiplicity correction.

Report heterogeneity across mice, laboratories and recording sessions.

Never treat spikes, trials, units or sessions as independent mice.

Test whether predictive improvements are stable rather than driven by one animal, one lab or increased model complexity.

Do not equate a model advantage with evidence of physical discrete neural atoms.
