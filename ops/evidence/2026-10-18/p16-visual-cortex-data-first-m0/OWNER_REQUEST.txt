## 7. VISUAL STIMULUS CONFOUNDING

This is the main scientific difference from P15.

Visual stimulation creates structured neural responses.

A predictive model using local neural history may simply recover visual stimulus timing or predictable image/movie sequences.

For each candidate stimulus family, assess:

- Exact stimulus identity
- Stimulus onset/offset times
- Repetition counts
- Frame/condition information
- Presentation order
- Interstimulus intervals
- Session drift
- Running/pupil coverage
- Potential habituation/adaptation
- Source timestamp quality

Evaluate whether a strong stimulus-conditioned baseline can be constructed.

Avoid models where a weak stimulus baseline makes local neural history appear artificially valuable.

A movie's next frame must not become a real-time predictor unless the experiment's predetermined stimulus schedule makes it genuinely available at the prediction time.

If a predictor uses retrospectively known stimulus or behavioral outcomes, explicitly identify it as an offline conditional analysis, not online forecasting.

Do not select a stimulus family based on favorable neural prediction scores.
